🎨 Polish Passes (subtle, not dramatic)

1. Speed Ramping (optional — high polish)

If a shot still feels slow after trimming, add a subtle 120% speed ramp in the MIDDLE of the action (the fast part), then ease back to 100% at the payoff moment. This makes the mechanical action feel snappier while the satisfying click/lock still lands at normal speed.

ShotSpeed Ramp
Tripod100% → 115% (legs falling) → 100% (lock click)
Mic100% → 120% (sliding in) → 100% (seat)
LightNo ramp. The bloom IS the payoff.
RecordNo ramp. Real-time press. Sacred.

2. Black Flash Between Cuts (2–3 frames)

Add 2–3 frames (0.06–0.1s) of pure black between each shot. This creates a subtle "blink" rhythm that makes the cuts feel intentional and punchy rather than jarring. It mimics a camera shutter closing between actions.

Shot 1 → [2 frames black] → Shot 2 → [2 frames black] → Shot 3 → [3 frames black] → Shot 4

The extra frame before the record shot creates a micro-beat of tension. Like a breath before the payoff.

3. Grade Polish

Keep the exact same red-isolated B&W. Add only:

  • Sharpen +5–10 — makes the metal and cable textures pop
  • Vignette 10–15% — subtle darkening of corners, draws eye to centre action
  • Grain 3–5% — adds organic film texture, removes the "phone video" feel
⚠️ Do NOT add: Glow effects, lens flares, motion blur, zoom transitions, or any filter that makes it look "edited." The goal is to look like it was shot by a professional, not edited by one.

4. Loop Point

The last frame of Shot 4 (record LED lit, still) should cut HARD back to the first frame of Shot 1 (tripod legs mid-drop). This creates the illusion of continuous preparation: record → reset → build → record. The loop should feel infinite, not cyclical.
